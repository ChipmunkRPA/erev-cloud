# Cost-recovery modification boundaries

Evidence revision 33 measures COST_RECOVERY at native modification boundaries using the
same stage-09 target that calculates revenue. Without an EAC, the target is cost recovery
capped by the allocation. With an approved EAC, stage 09 uses the existing cost-to-cost
calculation and scope rules. The boundary does not require or invent an EAC merely to
measure cost recovery. Prospective bases retain original costs and revenue so later costs
are not counted twice. Earlier evidence revisions keep their former boundary behavior.

Complete common-end-date continuation is now available for combined COST_RECOVERY through
the wizard, dated assessment and existing classification/approval flow. Unit, time and
input-method admission restrictions remain. The native application accepts the existing
prospective or cumulative-catch-up treatment for cost-recovery common-term changes; it
does not substitute its own classification.

Validation:
- 25 native reassessment cases pass. Cost recovery covers both treatments with/without EAC,
  original-member costs after the boundary, the allocation cap, unchanged prior-period
  schedules, exact trace replay and prior-revision refusal.
- A populated no-EAC workflow creates the reassessment, classifies it prospectively, previews,
  approves and applies it. The 108,000 allocation retains 300.00 cost-recovery income.
  Status, modification-register and causal-analysis reports replay with equal output hashes.
- Scoped backend/frontend types and lint pass.

These checks establish the combined common-term path and its native boundary reader, not
every ordinary, legacy, mixed-scope or acquired cost-recovery amendment scenario. First-EAC
arrival after a modification remains an additional transition scenario to verify. Divergent
terms, changed starts, combined quantity/term changes and member removal/splitting remain
unfinished. Percentage/milestone scope still awaits the owner's decision. No deployment.
